**Results**

**Overview of 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing data**
A total of 1,398,204 raw reads were generated across 11 samples (Table 1). After quality filtering, 519,769 reads (37.2% retention) remained, resulting in a final dataset of 161 amplicon sequence variants (ASVs). Taxonomic classification using the SILVA database successfully assigned 162 ASVs to at least the domain rank, with decreasing taxonomic resolution at higher ranks (Table S1).

**Sample composition and filtering**
The 11 samples included frost flower (n = 7) and sea-ice brine (n = 4) communities from an ice chamber experiment. After filtering, three samples contained chloroplast sequences (total ASVs: 1; reads: 26), which were retained separately for downstream analysis (Table S2). The prokaryotic dataset comprised 466,560 reads across all 11 samples.

**Taxonomic summary**
The most abundant phylum was Pseudomonadota (116 ASVs), followed by Bacteroidota (22 ASVs) and Campylobacterota (13 ASVs; Table 2). Verrucomicrobiota and Actinomycetota were represented by four and three ASVs, respectively. Taxonomic classification success decreased at higher ranks: 98.7% of ASVs were assigned to phylum, 96.3% to class, 94.1% to order, 92.5% to family, and 80.5% to genus (Table S1).

**Limitations**
A subset of ASVs could not be classified beyond the genus level, potentially impacting finer-scale community analysis. Chloroplast sequences were present in three samples but did not significantly affect prokaryotic analyses.
