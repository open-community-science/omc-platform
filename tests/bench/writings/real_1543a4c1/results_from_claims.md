# Results from claims (qwen/qwen3.6-35b-a3b)

The dataset comprised 735 amplicon sequence variants (ASVs) derived from 63 samples across two collection dates. A quality assessment noted that the two temporal groups were completely separated at the domain level. Samples collected on 2026-06-04 (n = 44) consisted of 100% Bacteria (Pseudomonadota: 76.38%, Campylobacterota: 19.25%, Bacteroidota: 3.83%), while samples from 2026-06-21 (n = 19) consisted of 100% Eukaryota (SAR: 62.01%, Archaeplastida: 30.66%, Amorphea: 6.58%). Although both groups were labeled as "16S amplicon sequencing frost flower" in the experiment metadata, zero ASVs were shared between the groups. All 418 domain-specific ASVs from the first group were bacterial, and all 317 domain-specific ASVs from the second group were eukaryotic. This complete domain separation indicates that the dataset likely combined 16S and 18S amplicon data without proper separation. Consequently, all downstream beta-diversity and differential abundance comparisons between these temporal groups were confounded by this taxonomic discrepancy.

Observed richness correlated strongly with sequencing depth (Spearman r = 0.6262, p < 0.001). Shannon diversity (r = 0.0573, p = 0.6553) and Simpson diversity (r = -0.0851, p = 0.5071) did not correlate with sequencing depth (p > 0.5 for both). Pielou evenness negatively correlated with sequencing depth (r = -0.4508, p = 0.0002).

Principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) of Bray-Curtis distances revealed a distinct separation along the first axis (PC1), which accounted for 34.09% of the variance, with PC2 explaining 14.18% (Figure 1). Samples from the first collection date clustered at negative PC1 values (-0.94 to -0.10), whereas samples from the second date clustered at positive PC1 values (0.76 to 1.60). Collection date explained 92% of the variance in PC1 (R² = 0.9203), resulting in complete separation between the two sampling dates. Library name explained 35% of PC1 variance (R² = 0.3495), while the temporal effect dominated over batch effects. Geographic distance correlated significantly with Bray-Curtis distance (Mantel r = 0.7843, p = 0.0).

Nearly all ASVs showed complete separation between the two temporal groups, with 48 ASVs yielding p-values of 0.0 in differential testing. ASVs enriched in the first group included ASV_000005 (mean abundance: 652.66), ASV_000003 (mean abundance: 705.16), and ASV_000004 (mean abundance: 663.52), all with zero mean abundance in the second group. Conversely, ASV_000001, ASV_000002, and ASV_000009 were exclusively present in the second group (mean abundance in group 1 = 0).

Within the bacterial group (44 samples), 25 ASVs were present in ≥50% of samples. The core community was dominated by marine-associated genera, including Colwellia (3 ASVs, phylum Pseudomonadota), Arcobacter (2 ASVs, Campylobacterota), Paraglaciecola (2 ASVs), Alteromonas, and Pseudoalteromonas. The top core ASVs were ASV_000005 (Alteromonas, prevalence = 1.0, mean abundance = 652.66), ASV_000010 (Pseudoalteromonas, prevalence = 1.0, mean abundance = 150.98), ASV_000003 (Arcobacter, prevalence = 0.95, mean abundance = 705.16), and ASV_000004 (Arcobacter, prevalence = 0.84, mean abundance = 663.52). Core phyla included Pseudomonadota, Campylobacterota, Actinomycetota, Bacteroidota, and Planctomycetota. Within the eukaryotic group (19 samples), 57 ASVs were present in ≥50% of samples. The core was dominated by the SAR clade and Archaeplastida, with top ASVs including ASV_000001 (SAR, prevalence = 1.0, mean abundance = 3414.11), ASV_000002 (Archaeplastida, prevalence = 1.0, mean abundance = 2399.53), and ASV_000009 (SAR, prevalence = 1.0, mean abundance = 513.05). Bacillariophytina appeared as core taxa across 4 ASVs, and 7 ASVs reached ≥90% prevalence (Table 1). The eukaryotic group contained zero transient taxa, with all 317 ASVs present in ≥10% of samples. In contrast, the bacterial group contained 230 transient taxa out of 418 total ASVs.
