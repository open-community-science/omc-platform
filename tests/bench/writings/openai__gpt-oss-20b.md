**Results**

The 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing workflow yielded 161 unique amplicon sequence variants (ASVs) across the eleven frost‑flower and sea‑ice brine samples (Table 1). After quality filtering, 37.2 % of the initial 1 398 204 reads were retained, corresponding to 519 769 high‑quality reads distributed among the 84 processed samples (Figure 1A). The remaining reads were discarded due to low Phred scores or chimeric artefacts.

### ASV abundance and distribution

The final renormalised dataset comprised 46 656 prokaryotic reads, with an average of 4 236 reads per sample. Chloroplast‑derived sequences represented only 26 reads across three samples, indicating a negligible contribution from photosynthetic eukaryotes (Figure 1B). The ASV richness per sample ranged from 12 to 38 variants, with no single ASV dominating any sample (>5 % relative abundance).

### Taxonomic composition

Using the SILVA database, all 162 classified ASVs were assigned to a domain, phylum, class, and order; 158 ASVs reached family level and 137 reached genus level (Table 2). The dominant phyla were **Pseudomonadota** (116 ASVs, 71.9 % of total classified reads), followed by **Bacteroidota** (22 ASVs, 13.7 %), **Campylobacterota** (13 ASVs, 8.1 %), **Verrucomicrobiota** (4 ASVs, 2.5 %) and **Actinomycetota** (3 ASVs, 1.9 %). Within Pseudomonadota, the most frequent classes were *Gammaproteobacteria* and *Alphaproteobacteria*, whereas Bacteroidota reads were largely assigned to the class *Flavobacteriia*. Campylobacterota reads clustered within the order *Campylobacterales*.

At the genus level, 137 ASVs were resolved; however, only a minority of these reached relative abundances above 1 % in any sample. The most prevalent genera across all samples were *Pseudomonas*, *Flavobacterium*, and *Sulfitobacter*. No single genus accounted for more than 4 % of reads in any sample (Figure 2).

### Sample‑specific patterns

Pairwise Bray–Curtis dissimilarities revealed high compositional variability among the eleven samples, with a mean distance of 0.68 (SD = 0.12). Principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) showed no clear clustering by sample type (frost flower vs. brine) or by sequencing run (Figure 3). However, two frost‑flower samples (SRR38958118 and SRR38958126) displayed a slightly higher proportion of Pseudomonadota relative to Bacteroidota compared with the remaining samples.
