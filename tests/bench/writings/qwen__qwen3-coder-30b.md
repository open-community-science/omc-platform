**Results**

The bacterial communities associated with frost flowers and sea-ice brine from an ice chamber experiment were characterized using 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing. A total of 11 samples were processed, representing 11 distinct SRA runs (Table 1). Following quality filtering and sequence normalization, a total of 161 ASVs were identified within the prokaryotic fraction, with 162 ASVs classified at taxonomic ranks using the SILVA database (Figure 1).

The overall sequencing effort yielded 519,769 reads following filtering, representing 37.2% of the input reads retained after quality filtering (Table 1). Of these, 46,656 reads were assigned to prokaryotic ASVs, while 26 reads were classified as chloroplast ASVs from three samples.

Taxonomic classification revealed that the dominant phyla within the bacterial community were Pseudomonadota (n = 116), Bacteroidota (n = 22), Campylobacterota (n = 13), Verrucomicrobiota (n = 4), and Actinomycetota (n = 3) (Figure 1). These phyla accounted for the majority of classified ASVs, with Pseudomonadota comprising the largest proportion at 71.6% of classified ASVs.

The taxonomic resolution varied across ranks, with 162 ASVs classified at domain, phylum, and class levels; 161 ASVs at order level; 158 ASVs at family level; and 137 ASVs at genus level (Table 1). The number of classified ASVs per rank reflects the completeness of taxonomic assignment across the dataset.

The dataset includes 11 samples from an ice chamber experiment, representing frost flowers and associated sea-ice brine. Sample metadata and sequencing statistics are summarized in Table 1. The ASV abundance table (seqtab_final) was generated after normalization, which included rarefaction and relative abundance calculations for downstream analyses (Figure 1).

The distribution of ASVs across samples is shown in Figure 1, illustrating the diversity and abundance patterns within each sample. Taxonomic assignments were made using the SILVA database, with taxonomic ranks resolved up to genus level for most ASVs.
