**Results**

The 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing workflow generated a total of 1,398,204 raw reads across the eleven ice‑chamber samples (Table 1). Following quality filtering, 519,769 reads were retained, representing a sequencing depth of 37.2 % of the original input (Figure 1; [CITE]). After removal of chloroplast‑derived sequences, the dataset comprised 161 amplicon‑sequence variants (ASVs) present in all 11 samples (Figure 2; [CITE]).

Taxonomic assignment using the SILVA database classified 162 of the 162 ASVs at the domain level, with complete assignment down to the family level for 158 ASVs and to the genus level for 137 ASVs (Table 2). The taxonomic composition was dominated by members of the phylum *Pseudomonadota*, which accounted for 116 of the total ASVs, followed by *Bacteroidota* (22 ASVs), *Campylobacterota* (13 ASVs), *Verrucomicrobiota* (4 ASVs), and *Actinomycetota* (3 ASVs) (Figure 3; [CITE]). All classified ASVs were assigned to these five phyla, indicating a highly concentrated community structure.

Rarefaction analyses revealed that the majority of ASVs were detected across multiple samples, with only a single ASV classified as chloroplast‑derived and present in three of the eleven samples (Table 3). The final renormalized dataset retained 161 prokaryotic ASVs across 11 samples, corresponding to 46,656 normalized reads; the chloroplast ASV subset comprised one ASV present in three samples and 26 normalized reads (Table 3; [CITE]).

These results provide a quantitative overview of the bacterial communities associated with frost flowers and brine within the ice‑chamber experiment, establishing the baseline composition and depth of sequencing required for downstream ecological analyses.
